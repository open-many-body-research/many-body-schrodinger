## What the historical artifacts establish

The Fock and hp notes explicitly separate ordinary numerical screening from interval certificates. Their finite-order width fits cannot establish an asymptotic approximation rate. Their trial families differ from the dyadic dictionary of Theorem T and must not be silently substituted into its statement.

The Schwartz family includes logarithms and negative powers of the radial sum. Its constant algebra is `Q[gamma, log 2, pi^2]`, unlike the three-dimensional rational span used for the exact dyadic Slater dictionary. Its finite-domain and moment arguments are useful historical mathematics, but neither their finite certificates nor observed slopes prove Theorem T's RATE premise.

The hp notes give a concrete proposed rational moment scheme with Duffy substitutions and reciprocal-series error bounds. They explicitly say that the production interval implementation and graph RATE were not completed in that pilot. The root audit does not change those statements or treat the pilot as a verified executable approximation algorithm.

The old `helium/select_residual_trial.py` contains a fixed target energy value used for exploratory candidate selection. The file explicitly excludes that value from certification. Its presence therefore does not invalidate a separately checked arbitrary rational trial, but the selector is not the asymptotic, prior-energy-free algorithm specified in `rwa_proof/THEOREM_T_COMPOSITION.md`. The older `select_trial.py` similarly uses Decimal inverse iteration to propose coefficients. No theorem about polynomial termination follows from either script.

The general-atom discussion in `research/analytic.md` carefully distinguishes a spectral infimum from an attained normalized ground state and includes the charge/input length in a meaningful uniform complexity question. Its Lieb ionization-bound argument is background for the extension scope, not a hardness theorem for binding atoms. The hydrogen spin example also correctly illustrates that energy alone does not specify ground-state observables.
