# Approximation audit for the two-electron continuum target

Research date: 2026-09-09. This is a targeted primary-source audit, not an exhaustive bibliography. Its target is the fixed-two-electron, one/two-nucleus, bounded-charge, separated-geometry, promised-gap family in [tractable_target.md](../nogo/tractable_target.md). No theorem in this note has been Lean-checked. “Paper theorem” below means the stated implication with its actual hypotheses, not an independent certification of every step of the published proof.

The strongest verified ingredients are constructive exponential **approximation** for weighted analytic functions with isolated point singularities on bounded low-dimensional domains, and constructive exponential **quadrature** for a specified class of singular simplex integrals. I did not verify a paper combining these into a uniform polynomial-bit-cost solver for the original unbounded six-dimensional helium/H₂ operator. This is a scope finding, not a proof that such a solver cannot exist.

## 1. Hylleraas completeness and angular CI are different assertions

**Klahn–Bingel I (1977), DOI [10.1007/BF00548026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00548026), Theoretica Chimica Acta 44, 9–26. PRIMARY ABSTRACT ONLY.** The publisher explicitly reports sufficient and necessary/sufficient Rayleigh–Ritz convergence criteria, and a §9 example showing that L² completeness alone does not imply energy convergence. The subscription full text was inaccessible in this audit. I therefore do not transcribe a theorem statement, claim to have checked its proof, or infer an effective convergence rate from the abstract.

**Klahn–Bingel II (1977), DOI [10.1007/BF00548027](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00548027), 44, 27–43. PRIMARY ABSTRACT ONLY.** The publisher specifically identifies Theorem 10 as proving Rayleigh–Ritz convergence for a Hylleraas basis in the coordinates (s,t,u), and discusses orbital-exponent conditions for Slater/Gaussian systems. The full theorem and proof could not be read because the publisher offered subscription access only. This verifies the existence and advertised subject of Theorem 10, but does not verify its exact exponent/index restrictions, topology, a quantitative modulus, or uniformity. Secondary lecture notes quoting a completeness theorem were found but not substituted for the primary text. Consequently no part of A is discharged by this audit of Klahn–Bingel.

**Hill (1985), DOI [10.1063/1.449481](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.449481), J. Chem. Phys. 83, 1173–1196. FULL TEXT NOT OBTAINED.** The bibliographic identity is corroborated in the primary Goddard paper below; Hill's exact hypotheses and proof were not independently read. The frequently cited angular-momentum asymptotic must not be presented as a theorem about finite Hylleraas-polynomial degree. Hill's different 1995 paper, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevA.51.4433](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.51.4433), PRA 51, 4433–4471, has an accessible primary abstract describing uniform asymptotic expansions for a nonlinear scale parameter and three model problems. Its full text was also unavailable; no general helium convergence theorem is inferred from that abstract.

**Goddard (2009), DOI [10.1137/080727956](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/080727956), SIAM J. Math. Anal. 41, 77–116. PAPER THEOREM, CONDITIONAL.** [Full publisher PDF in the author's institutional repository](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/16365307/1_G_JMA.pdf) was read, especially pp. 78–83, Theorem 3.1, Lemma 4.1, §5.2. Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
E_L-E=2\pi^2L^{-3}\int_0^\infty r^5|\psi(r,r,0)|^2\,dr+o(L^{-3}).
\]

Its assumptions include existence of derivatives in (r_{12}) through order four, regularity and envelopes of their boundary traces (A_j) and radial derivatives, coalescence-trace moments, and weighted integrability of (I_3,I_4). The energy conclusion needs only third-order regularity as explained on p. 82; that page expressly leaves justification of the requisite regularity assumptions unresolved. Page 78 emphasizes that (V_L), although angularly truncated, has unrestricted radial functions and is infinite dimensional. The coefficient depends on the unknown exact wavefunction; the little-o remainder has no computable uniform modulus here. The paper critiques rigor in Hill's earlier derivation on p. 79. These are conditional algebraic angular-CI asymptotics, not an exponential finite-basis construction or a certified stopping rule.

**Schwartz (2006), [arXiv:math-ph/0605018](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0605018). EMPIRICAL.** The complete three-page preprint was read. Its F basis uses (e^{-ks/2}s^l(u/s)^m(t/s)^n), optionally multiplied by (log s), with even (n) and a degree cutoff. At order 50 it has 24,099 functions and reports roughly fifty digits of helium energy. The final extra digits are explicitly extrapolated from successive differences. This is an excellent illustration of the practical accuracy to be explained, but this short report contains no computable tail theorem, rigorous rounding enclosure, or bit-complexity proof. Its negative powers and logarithms also prevent silently identifying it with the polynomial Hylleraas basis of a different theorem.

## 2. What the Flad/Schneider wavelet papers actually prove

**Flad–Hackbusch–Schneider (2006), DOI [10.1051/m2an:2006007](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1051/m2an:2006007/), ESAIM M2AN 40, 49–61. PAPER THEOREM, CONDITIONAL.** Full text read, §1.1, §2, Lemma 1 (p. 53), and subsequent coefficient estimates. Assuming orbital asymptotic smoothness (|\partial^\beta\phi(x)|\le c_\beta|x-R|^{1-|\beta|}), Lemma 1 places orbitals in tensor Besov approximation classes. The resulting best-N-term statement allows every algebraic exponent, using sufficiently regular wavelets. The objects are HF/DFT orbitals and finite-rank one-electron density matrices, on bounded neighborhoods/cubes. It is not a theorem about exact correlated many-electron wavefunctions. The constants (c_\beta), wavelet regularity, and approximation constants vary with order; “every algebraic rate” does not supply an exponential modulus. To see the logical distinction independently, (a_N=e^{-(\log(N+1))^2}) decays faster than every power but slower than (e^{-cN^\theta}) for every (c,\theta>0).

**Flad–Hackbusch–Schneider (2007), DOI [10.1051/m2an:2007016](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1051/m2an:2007016/), ESAIM M2AN 41, 261–279. PAPER THEOREM, CONDITIONAL.** Full text read, Assumptions 1.1/1.2 (pp. 264–265), Lemmas 2.1/2.5 and Corollary 2.4 (pp. 267–269). The object is a two-particle Jastrow correlation function with assumed derivative bounds near pair/nuclear cusps. On bounded (Omega\times\Omega), the hybrid wavelet basis yields best-N-term (H^1) approximation classes with exponent (1/q-1/2), (q>1); positive exponents are below (1/2). Corollary 2.4 uses (|x-y|) to show a sharp Besov obstruction for (q\le1) for that representation. The text expressly states that the ansatz error is generally uncontrolled, its regularity assumption lacks a general justification, matrix-construction complexity is outside scope, and exterior behavior is excluded. This restricted representation obstruction does not rule out other correlated bases or hp spaces. It establishes neither a non-density theorem for all cusped ansätze nor a universal complexity obstruction.

**Flad–Schneider–Schulze (2008), DOI [10.1002/mma.1021](https://doi.org/10.1002/mma.1021), Math. Methods Appl. Sci. 31, 2172–2201. PAPER THEOREM.** The complete [author preprint dated 2007-05-23](https://d-nb.info/1218381248/34) was read, especially Definition 1 and Theorems 1–2 (preprint pp. 4–6), and their bootstrap proofs §§2.2–2.3. Its third author is **Schulze, not Schwab**. It concerns HF orbitals on the infinite cone (mathbb R_+\times S^2). Theorem 1 preserves specified asymptotic expansions through a shifted Fock eigenproblem; Theorem 2 transfers this property to SCF limits produced by level shifting from an initial guess with the same property. The asymptotic class controls polar expansions and rapid exterior decay. It is not an hp convergence or full correlated six-dimensional Schrödinger theorem; orderwise smooth asymptotics alone does not specify factorial derivative constants or a computable exponential rate.

**Flad–Schneider (2012), DOI [10.1051/m2an/2011077](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1051/m2an/2011077/), ESAIM M2AN 46, 1055–1080. PAPER THEOREM.** Full text read, particularly Theorems 2.6–2.8 (pp. 1061–1063), §§2.4–2.5. Theorem 2.7 proves (s^*)-compressibility of the preconditioned HF Galerkin matrix: combined nuclear/Hartree potential (s^*=(s+1)/3), exchange (s^*=s/3), with wavelet regularity/vanishing moments and orbital asymptotic-smoothness assumptions. Page 1062 restricts to a fixed ball and neglects exterior contributions. Page 1063 explicitly calls these conditions necessary but insufficient for an optimal adaptive nonlinear HF algorithm. Thus even “optimal complexity” here must not be promoted to a completed global algorithm, bit-cost bound, or correlated continuum certificate.

## 3. Constructive exponential hp results and a quadrature ingredient

**Feischl–Schwab (2020), DOI [10.1007/s00211-019-01085-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00211-019-01085-z), Numer. Math. 144, 323–346. PAPER THEOREM.** The [2018 full preprint](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2018-23.pdf) was read: weighted spaces §2, mesh construction §3, Theorem 1 (printed p. 9), proof §4.2. For a bounded polytope in (d=2,3), finite isolated singular points, admissible weights, and weighted Gevrey regularity (delta\ge1), it constructs conforming hp projectors with

\[
\|u-\Pi u\|_{H^1}\le C e^{-bN^{1/(1+\delta d)}},\qquad
N=O(p^{d+1/\delta}).
\]

For (delta=1), this is (N=O(|\log\varepsilon|^{d+1})). The proof constructs local projections, face/edge liftings, and low-order terminal-layer approximants. Constants depend on weighted regularity and mesh parameters. The near-coalescence remark (p. 8) does establish uniform geometric constants with respect to point separation; it does not establish uniform regularity constants for our physical family. The theorem's hypotheses concern isolated points; helium in (mathbb R^6) has intersecting positive-dimensional collision sets. The final discussion suggests higher-dimensional point extensions, but this does not remove that distinction. No unbounded exterior or bit arithmetic is analyzed.

**Maday–Marcati, [arXiv:1912.07483v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07483v2) (2022 revision). PAPER THEOREMS.** Full text read, §§2–5 and numerical Remark 8. Section 2.1 uses the flat torus (mathbb R^d/(Lmathbb Z)^d), (d=2,3). The potential lies in (L^{p_V}), (p_V>\max(1,d/2)), and a weighted analytic class (K^{\omega,\infty}_{-2+\epsilon}), with a finite point-singularity set. Theorem 1 gives eventual dG quasioptimality under assumptions (6a–d),(7a–b). Theorem 2 (p. 22) proves weighted analytic regularity for (-\Delta u+Vu+|u|^{n-1}u=\lambda u), (n=2,3,4). Theorem 3 (p. 31) gives eigenpair errors (Ce^{-bN^{1/(d+1)}}). Constants and the sufficiently-fine threshold are existential, not a numerical certificate. Section 6 changes to Dirichlet boundaries for experiments and expressly distinguishes that setting from the analysis. This is a nonlinear low-dimensional model, not the full two-electron Coulomb equation. The journal DOI 10.1142/S0218202523500586 appeared in secondary metadata but its primary landing page was inaccessible; the verified citation used here is the arXiv version.

**Chernov–von Petersdorff–Schwab (2011), DOI [10.1051/m2an/2010061](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1051/m2an/2010061/), ESAIM M2AN 45, 387–422. PAPER THEOREM.** Full text read, Assumptions 3.1–3.2/4.1, transformations §3.3, Theorems 4.2/4.5 (pp. 408–409), and Proposition 2.8. For integrals on simplex pairs with a prescribed algebraic diagonal singularity and weighted Gevrey-(delta) derivative bounds, a constructive coordinate transformation isolates the singularity. Theorem 4.5 gives error (Ce^{-rN^{1/(2d\delta+1)}}) with (N) function evaluations. Remark 4.6 improves this to exponent (1/(2d\delta)) when the transformation completely removes the singularity. This supplies a genuine Coulomb-integration building block. However a function-evaluation count is not a bit-operation count: node/weight generation, certified evaluation, dependence on hp degree and element geometry, and total matrix norm error still need analysis for C. No conclusion about the physical eigenfunction's approximability follows from this quadrature theorem.

## 4. A recent apparent answer rejected after reading

**Mohammadi (March 2026), ResearchGate author-uploaded preprint DOI [10.13140/RG.2.2.24365.35044](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403062081_Explicit_Convergence_Bounds_for_Correlated_Hylleraas_Basis_Sets). UNVERIFIED CLAIMS; NOT USED AS A THEOREM.** Full author-uploaded text was inspected, especially §§4–7. It claims explicit universal Hylleraas error constants. Load-bearing defects prevent using that claim: §6 says the exact helium state is outside (H^2), contrary to the Coulomb operator domain; applies Parseval to the nonorthogonal Hylleraas expansion without Gram factors; and §5 takes a regularity parameter to its endpoint without controlling its constants. The claimed equality of Sobolev norms under a linear coordinate change ignores derivative transformation matrices. The explicit-constant proof includes unresolved equation references and unfinished algebra. These are mathematical reasons to withhold validation, independently of its publication status. In particular this source cannot discharge A–D or refute faster convergence of other bases.

## 5. Mapping to A–D: remaining obligations are precise

These are deductions from the scopes above and the target specification, not additional published theorems.

| Target hypothesis | Verified useful ingredient | What remains for the original physical family |
|---|---|---|
| **A: effective uniform exponential H¹ approximation** | Weighted Gevrey-to-hp approximation on bounded point-singularity domains; conditional angular-CI asymptotics; advertised Hylleraas completeness | Prove weighted analytic/Gevrey bounds adapted to all pair, nuclear, and intersecting collision strata in six dimensions, with uniform computable constants; control the exterior with an effective radius and derivative/tail estimates; obtain a certified modulus. Completeness alone supplies no modulus. |
| **B: poly(L,p) construction and dimension** | Feischl–Schwab explicitly construct hp meshes/projectors and dimension bounds for their domain class | Produce and encode the corresponding six-dimensional or rigorously reduced-coordinate conforming spaces uniformly in nuclear input; count coefficients, charts and mesh layers; control coordinate-map degeneracies. The existence of good approximants does not compute their coefficients. |
| **C: certified matrices in poly(L,p,s)** | Singular simplex-pair quadrature with exponential evaluation-count bounds | Track all matrix entries, basis degree, mesh scales, Gram conditioning, singular transformations, node/weight approximation, rounding, and global operator-norm enclosure. These obligations cannot be replaced by “analytic integrals” or arbitrary-precision floating point. |
| **D: certified smallest generalized eigenpair** | Variational/quasioptimal convergence provides the analytic comparison | Give a finite bit-algorithm, prove index identification and perturbation bounds for enclosed matrices and Gram factors, and connect its trial state to the original continuum form. This finite-matrix step cannot supply the missing continuum approximation bound. |

An exact symmetry reduction may reduce the coordinate count for helium, but its measure, kinetic form, singular boundary strata, spin-sector identification, and tail must first be proved equivalent to the original problem. A linear transformation between distance coordinates does not turn their physical measure or H¹ form into unweighted Euclidean norms.

Conforming (C^0) finite elements belong naturally to the quadratic-form domain (H^1), not necessarily the operator domain (H^2). Their elementwise Laplacian alone omits interface distributions. Any certificate using an (L^2) residual must prove the required operator-domain membership or replace it with a valid form/dual-residual theorem including interface terms.

The precise open bridge exposed by these papers is the target's A–D implication instantiated for the fixed-two-electron physical family, with all constants computable uniformly in the stated geometry and gap promises. This audit proves neither that bridge nor its negation.
