# Complexity boundary: primary-source audit

Research date: 2026-09-09 UTC. Requested update window: 2024-09-09 through 2026-09-09. This document records primary sources inspected, not an exhaustive literature theorem. Status labels below distinguish published proofs, preprint claims, and independent deductions; none of the cited complexity reductions has been Lean-checked in this project.

## Foundational results and their exact scope

**PROVEN (paper proof only): Kempe–Kitaev–Regev.** The promise problem for a polynomial-size sum of 2-local Hermitian terms on qubits, with polynomially bounded norms and an inverse-polynomial separation between the YES and NO energy thresholds, is QMA-complete. It is a worst-case finite-dimensional result. It is not itself a theorem about Coulomb Hamiltonians. The source supplies two proofs, including a perturbative construction. Source: *The Complexity of the Local Hamiltonian Problem*, arXiv:quant-ph/0406180; DOI 10.1137/S0097539704445226. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406180), [journal](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/S0097539704445226).

**PROVEN (paper proof only): O'Gorman–Irani–Whitfield–Fefferman.** Let the input include finitely many concisely specified spatial orbitals, an electron number, and energy thresholds separated by an inverse polynomial. The orbital description must permit efficient evaluation of kinetic, external-potential, and Coulomb integrals. Distinguishing the lowest energies of the resulting Hamiltonian on the fixed electron-number sector is QMA-complete. Hardness is encoded in constructed Gaussian orbital combinations, through antiferromagnetic Heisenberg → Hubbard → electronic-structure reductions. The 2022 journal version also permits nuclear attraction from positive **unit** point charges (Appendix D, Corollary 1). This extension is absent from the arXiv v1 inspected. The orbitals remain part of the input and impose a finite-dimensional restriction. The journal explicitly leaves unrestricted continuum hardness and hardness with controlled basis-set error open (Section V). It does not restrict to a specified total-spin sector. Source: *Intractability of Electronic Structure in a Fixed Basis*, arXiv:2103.08215; DOI 10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.020322. [Journal PDF, especially pp. 3–7 and Appendix D](https://journals.aps.org/prxquantum/pdf/10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.020322), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08215).

**PROVEN (paper proof only): Schuch–Verstraete.** The construction uses a tunable spatial spin magnetic field in addition to an engineered electrostatic potential. It reduces a 2D local spin problem to a Heisenberg lattice with fields, then to half-filled Hubbard, then to a continuum electronic model. The supplementary continuum construction uses delta-well lattice potentials; its final remarks identify the functional result specifically with spin-density functional theory, omitting orbital magnetic coupling. The functional is an ensemble constrained-search functional, enabling a convex-optimization reduction. Thus an efficient universal evaluation procedure with the requisite accuracy would solve QMA problems efficiently. This does not supply a reduction to the user's nonmagnetic, positive-point-nuclei-only Hamiltonian. Source: *Computational Complexity of interacting electrons and fundamental limitations of Density Functional Theory*, arXiv:0712.0483; DOI 10.1038/nphys1370. [Complete preprint including supplement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.0483), [journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys1370).

**PROVEN (paper proof only): Liu–Christandl–Verstraete.** Given a normalized two-fermion density matrix on a finite one-particle space of dimension polynomial in N, distinguish exact ensemble N-representability from trace-norm distance at least inverse-polynomial to every representable matrix. This promise problem is QMA-complete under polynomial-time oracle/Turing reductions. The reduction encodes qubits into pairs of fermionic modes and uses convex optimization. It is not a claim that every 1-RDM representability problem is hard, nor that arbitrary two-body fermionic coefficients have automatically been realized by nuclear Coulomb integrals. Source: *Quantum Computational Complexity of the N-Representability Problem: QMA Complete*, arXiv:quant-ph/0609125; DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.110503. [Primary preprint, pp. 2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0609125).

## Update-window findings

**PROVEN (paper proof only), restricted marginal problem.** Liebert et al., *Solving one-body ensemble N-representability problems with spin*, arXiv:2412.01805v2; DOI 10.22331/q-2025-12-02-1921, published 2025-12-02. The subject is orbital one-body representability with spin and ensemble-spectrum information, using representation theory, convex analysis, and discrete geometry. It does not replace the two-body representability problem above. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01805v2), [journal](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-12-02-1921/).

**PROVEN (preprint paper proof only), approximation rather than exact energies.** Stroeks, Terhal, and Herasymenko, *Optimizing fermionic Hamiltonians with classical interactions*, arXiv:2510.02122v2, updated 2026-02-23. For finite-mode hopping plus repulsive density-density interactions, the authors prove existence of Gaussian states with approximation ratio 1/3 under a traceless maximum-energy convention. Efficient constructions require additional structure, such as bipartite classical interactions. Their particle-number constraint in Theorem 2.7 fixes particle number **in expectation**; the exact particle-number version is explicitly left open. Constant-factor approximation does not imply arbitrary additive precision. [Primary full text, especially Problems 2.1 and 2.6, Theorems 2.2–2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02122v2).

**PROVEN (paper proof only) plus EMPIRICAL, impurity models.** Erakovic et al., *High Ground State Overlap via Quantum Embedding Methods*, arXiv:2408.01940; DOI 10.1103/PRXLife.3.013003, journal publication 2025-01-14. Rigorous analysis treats models in which only a constant number of orbitals participate in two-body interactions, with conditions on a guiding embedding or the free one-body gap. Numerical guiding-state overlaps in biomolecular embeddings are empirical. Neither part establishes a general continuum guarantee or polynomial dependence on log(1/epsilon). [Primary journal paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXLife.3.013003), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01940).

**Conditional method, not a general ground-state theorem.** Schleich et al., *Chemically motivated simulation problems are efficiently solvable on a quantum computer*, arXiv:2401.09268; DOI 10.1039/D5DD00377F, published online 2025-11-27, journal issue 2026. The abstract expressly makes assumptions and describes heuristically guided scattering-state preparation for dynamics. The title must not be read as a universal efficient ground-state solver. [Primary journal PDF](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2025/dd/d5dd00377f), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09268).

**EMPIRICAL/algorithmic proposal, no verified polynomial worst-case cost.** Massaccesi et al., *Determining the N-Representability of a Reduced Density Matrix via Unitary Evolution and Stochastic Sampling*, DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.4c01166, published 2024-11-14; arXiv:2503.17303, posted 2025-03-21. The differing dates reflect journal publication before arXiv posting. It proposes replacing explicit constraints with a hybrid quantum-stochastic procedure. The source does not establish a general polynomial-time solution of the QMA-complete promise problem. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17303), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.4c01166).

**Claimed theorem in a preprint, limited scope; complexity details not independently audited.** Schouten–Mazziotti, *Entanglement Complexity in Many-body Systems from Positivity Scaling Laws*, arXiv:2509.02944. Fixed-level exactness in a positivity hierarchy is related to polynomial-sized optimization. This is a sufficient representation assumption, not a proof that general Coulomb ground states satisfy a uniform fixed level, and not an exhaustive structural classification. Full bit-complexity and conditioning details have not been independently verified here. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02944v1).

No update-window primary source establishing QMA-hardness for the exact unrestricted nonmagnetic positive-point-nuclei Hamiltonian was located. This is a search outcome, not proof that no such source exists.

## Independent logical deductions: what the boundary actually says

The following is a complete elementary reduction argument, conditional on the cited finite-basis hardness theorem. Suppose a deterministic algorithm returns e with |e−E|≤epsilon for every encoded finite-basis instance, in time polynomial in full input bit length L and log(1/epsilon). For a promise instance with E≤a or E≥b and gap g=b−a>0, request epsilon=g/4 and accept iff e<(a+b)/2. In the YES case e≤a+g/4<(a+b)/2; in the NO case e≥b−g/4>(a+b)/2. Since g is inverse-polynomial, both the requested precision length and the computation are polynomial. Compose with the polynomial-time reductions from every QMA language. Then QMA is contained in P, hence P=QMA. A bounded-error quantum algorithm gives QMA=BQP instead. Neither separation is known unconditionally.

No such conclusion about the continuum model follows merely by omitting the projection. For any self-adjoint semibounded H and nonzero trial subspace S contained in its form domain,

    E_cont := inf_{||psi||=1} q_H[psi] <= inf_{psi in S, ||psi||=1} q_H[psi] =: E_S.

Proof: the first infimum ranges over a superset. This supplies only one inequality; a reduction needs a quantitative converse or equivalent spectral embedding. Counterexample in finite dimension: on C^2, H=diag(0,1) and S=span(e_2) yield E_cont=0 and E_S=1. Consequently, a certified basis error small relative to the promise gap is substantive work, not an implicit consequence of the variational principle.

Likewise, the existence of a “closed form” alone does not imply a polynomial algorithm. One must specify the expression language, representation size, construction procedure, and evaluation complexity. Complexity hardness does not rule out arbitrary finite parameterizations, special functions whose evaluation is hard, exponential-length formulae, or implicit characterizations. A classification of solvable subclasses is also not ruled out by QMA-hardness; the hardness theorem simply requires that any universal polynomial class include additional consequences or exclude the hard families. “Physically relevant” must be a mathematically specified predicate before an exact classification can be formulated.

The literal runtime requested in the problem omits M and the bit lengths of charges and positions. A coherent uniform bit-complexity theorem must include the full input length (or explicitly bound it as a polynomial in N), and must specify allowed observables and the representation of a selected ground state when degeneracy occurs.

## Honest small-instance demonstration boundary

The two-site, two-electron Hubbard model provides a concrete demonstration of the Heisenberg–Hubbard stage. Its singlet sector includes the block [[0,−2t],[−2t,U]], a further singlet has energy U, and three triplets have energy zero. For U>0,

    E = (U − sqrt(U^2+16t^2))/2,
    0 <= E + 4t^2/U
       = 64t^4 / [U (U+sqrt(U^2+16t^2))^2]
       <= 16t^4/U^3.

The identity follows by rationalizing the square root; the last inequality uses sqrt(U^2+16t^2)>=U. This makes superexchange and its error explicit. It demonstrates one finite reduction stage. It does not implement the subsequent Gaussian-orbital embedding, establish a complexity lower bound by itself, or demonstrate a continuum reduction. A report must label these limits prominently.

## Precise remaining target

Establish or refute the following existence statement: there are constants c>0 and polynomials p,q, and a polynomial-time map from every promise 2-local Hamiltonian instance x to positive rational nuclear charges, rational positions, electron number N, thresholds A<B, and a nonmagnetic continuum Born–Oppenheimer Coulomb Hamiltonian H_x, all with total encoding length at most p(|x|), such that B−A>=1/q(|x|), YES instances satisfy inf spec(H_x)<=A, and NO instances satisfy inf spec(H_x)>=B. All spectral infima are on the full antisymmetric continuum Hilbert space, with no externally supplied finite orbital restriction. A useful stronger version requires a binding margin E_{N-1}(x)−E_N(x)>=1/q(|x|), so the target energy is an isolated bound ground level. This stronger binding condition is an additional research target, not an established consequence.
