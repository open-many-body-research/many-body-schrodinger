One draft wording gap in the N=1 bit-cost paragraph was identified and corrected before the document was finalized. A general rational matrix with unrelated entry denominators cannot be assigned the stated minor-size bound merely from each entry's original bit length. The actual generated midpoint and bisection-shift matrices are dyadic. The final text explicitly chooses a single common denominator and defines L after this rescaling, including the current bisection midpoint. On that stated cost model, Schur entries are ratios of minors, Hadamard bounds their operand sizes by O(m(L+log m)), and O(m³) rational updates with a conservative cubic arithmetic bound give O(m⁶(L+log m)³) per PSD decision. This is only a finite-stage cost bound; the document does not extend it to whole-program efficient complexity.

The strict-margin argument for N=1 precision doubling is also valid as written. For each fixed finite grid, interval evaluation converges while the exact spatial contribution is at most t/4. The exact test that each entry radius is at most t must therefore succeed after finitely many doublings. An explicit advance formula for the final bit precision is not required for this termination implication; its absence prevents inferring a quantified overall bit-cost bound. The document makes that distinction.
