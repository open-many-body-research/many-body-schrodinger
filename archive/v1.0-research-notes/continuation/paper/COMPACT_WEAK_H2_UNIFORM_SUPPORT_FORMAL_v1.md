# Uniform-support compact weak H² approximation

Evidence category: fully formalized mathematical result. All six source modules compile in Lean 4.34.0-rc2 with the pinned Mathlib revision d9ed2b07e3d851ae48dbfe62550f6da9a1c128c9. Two expanded-statement and axiom audits cover 34 declarations; only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound occur.

Let E be any finite-dimensional real inner-product space with Lebesgue measure, f in L²(E;C), and d(v), e(v,w) actual L² witnesses satisfying every real C∞ compact-test weak derivative identity D_v f=d(v), D_w d(v)=e(v,w). Suppose f vanishes almost everywhere outside a ball of radius R. The explicit normalized bump sequence of outer radii 2/(n+1) produces smooth functions u_n with support in the one fixed closed ball of radius R+2. Their actual L² representatives and all first and ordered second directional derivatives converge strongly to f, d(v), and e(v,w), respectively. The same n is used for every derivative. No approximation, density, derivative existence beyond the stated actual weak H² hypotheses, or convergence theorem is assumed as an additional premise.

The construction first tests the weak derivative equation with translated reflected compact kernels, proving pointwise commutation of smooth convolution with each weak direction. Generic Young bounds give contraction, and the proved a.e.-convergence-plus-contraction Hilbert-space lemma gives strong L² convergence. Compact support follows directly from kernel support and a.e. input support; it does not assume pointwise compact support of an arbitrary L² quotient representative. A supplementary theorem proves that mollification preserves the genuine weak derivative graph itself.

For the ordinary product Y×T of arbitrary finite Euclidean factors, the exact WithLp 2 continuous linear equivalence and Mathlib's measure-preserving product-Lebesgue identity transport all weak tests and first/second classical chain rules. Given any compact K and an actual weak H² input vanishing a.e. off K, the theorem produces one compact L containing K and a C∞ sequence supported in L with every asserted product-space L² convergence. The maximum norm on the ordinary product is retained; no false Euclidean norm identity is used.

These are analytic existence and convergence results, not an executable numerical procedure. They do not by themselves prove a weak Grushin estimate, a quantitative approximation rate, analyticity, or full Theorem T.
