# Exact remainder equation and its blow-up

All identities in this file are **PROVEN by paper algebra**. They use the
actual ground-state equation and the previously verified extraction, not a
formal Fock expansion. They do not, on their own, prove analytic estimates.

## Physical domain and coordinates

Fix an integer \(Z\ge2\), and let \(\psi=\psi_Z\) be the normalized positive,
rotation-invariant, exchange-symmetric spatial ground state of
\[
H_Z=-\tfrac12(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)-Z/r-Z/s+1/u,\qquad
D(H_Z)=H^2(\mathbb R^6)\cap L^2_{\rm sym}.
\]
Its spin singlet is understood. Write \(E=E_Z\), \(\psi_0=\psi_Z(0)\),
\(\kappa=Z(2-\pi)/(3\pi)\), and
\[
r=b+c,\quad s=a+c,\quad u=a+b,\quad
S=a+b+2c,\quad T=\rho^2=r^2+s^2,\quad
q=(r^2+s^2-u^2)/2=c(S-c)-ab.
\]
The half-perimetric domain is the closed octant. Its reduced physical
measure is \(16\pi^2w\,da\,db\,dc\), where \(w=rsu\).
For differential identities start on its interior; boundary statements
mean the Cartesian weak identities or the actual reduced analytic
extensions where established. No boundary condition is added.

Define the homogeneous degree-zero coefficients
\[
\mathsf A=\frac{ac}{ru},\qquad
\mathsf B=\frac{bc}{su},\qquad
\Theta=1-\mathsf A-\mathsf B=\frac{abS}{rsu},
\]
and
\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+\mathsf A-\mathsf B&0&-\mathsf A\\
0&1-\mathsf A+\mathsf B&-\mathsf B\\
-\mathsf A&-\mathsf B&\mathsf A+\mathsf B
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf b=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1/r+1/s+2/u\\
1/r-1/s+2/u\\
1/r+1/s-2/u
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{PDE1}
\]
Then the physical Laplacian on invariant functions is
\[
\mathcal L=G_{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+\mathbf b\cdot\nabla
=w^{-1}\operatorname{div}(wG\nabla).
\tag{PDE2}
\]
Repeated indices in this file are summed over \(a,b,c\); thus off-diagonal
terms occur twice. One verification begins in distance coordinates:
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)F={}&F_{rr}+F_{ss}+2F_{uu}
+2F_r/r+2F_s/s+4F_u/u\\
&+\frac{r^2+u^2-s^2}{ru}F_{ru}
+\frac{s^2+u^2-r^2}{su}F_{su}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed linear change of coordinates gives (PDE1)–(PDE2).

## Conjugation cancels the explicit Coulomb zeroth-order potential

Put
\[
f=-ZS+u/2=(1/2-Z)(a+b)-2Zc,\qquad
v=\nabla f=(1/2-Z,1/2-Z,-2Z)^T.
\]
Since \(f\) is linear in the reduced coordinates,
\[
\mathcal Lf=\mathbf b\cdot v=-2Z/r-2Z/s+2/u=2V,
\qquad V=-Z/r-Z/s+1/u.
\tag{PDE3}
\]
Set \(\mathbf t=Gv\). Its components are completely explicit:
\[
\begin{aligned}
t_a&=1/2-Z+(Z+1/2)\mathsf A+(Z-1/2)\mathsf B,\\
t_b&=1/2-Z+(Z-1/2)\mathsf A+(Z+1/2)\mathsf B,\\
t_c&=-(Z+1/2)(\mathsf A+\mathsf B).
\end{aligned}
\tag{PDE4}
\]
The product rule gives
\[
e^{-f}(H_Z-E)e^f
=-\tfrac12\mathcal L-\mathbf t\cdot\nabla+W=:\mathcal P,
\]
\[
W=V-\tfrac12\mathcal Lf-\tfrac12v^TGv-E
=-Z^2-\tfrac14+Z\Theta-E.
\tag{PDE5}
\]
For example \(v^TGv=2Z^2+1/2-2Z\Theta\), either by multiplying the matrix
or computing \(|\nabla_{x_1,x_2}f|^2\) directly.

The cancellation of \(V-\mathcal Lf/2\) is exact. It does not remove the
first-order \(1/r,1/s,1/u\) coefficients of \(\mathcal L\), nor turn \(G\)
into a uniformly elliptic matrix.

## Subtraction of the actual quadratic logarithm

Let \(J=q\log T\). The previously established physical extraction is
\[
e^{-f}\psi=\kappa\psi_0J+\mathcal R.
\]
It follows that
\[
\boxed{\mathcal P\widehat{\mathcal R}=-\kappa\psi_0\mathcal PJ.}
\tag{PDE6}
\]
The source can be written without unspecified derivatives. In Cartesian
coordinates \(q=x_1\cdot x_2\) is harmonic and homogeneous of degree two,
so in dimension six
\[
\mathcal LJ=16q/T,\qquad
\mathbf t\cdot\nabla q=-ZqS/(rs)-u/2,\qquad
\mathbf t\cdot\nabla\log T=2f/T.
\tag{PDE7}
\]
The first identity follows from
\(\Delta\log|x|^2=8/|x|^2\) and \(x\cdot\nabla q=2q\).
For the second, dot
\((-Zx_1/r+(x_1-x_2)/(2u),-Zx_2/s-(x_1-x_2)/(2u))\)
with \((x_2,x_1)\). The last identity uses Euler homogeneity of \(f\).
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P\widehat{\mathcal R}
=\kappa\psi_0\left[
\frac{(8+2f)q}{T}
+\left(-\frac{ZqS}{rs}-\frac u2-Wq\right)\log T
\right].
}
\tag{PDE8}
\]
The principal part is \(-G_{ij}\partial_i\partial_j/2\), the first-order
coefficient is \(-\mathbf b/2-\mathbf t\), and the zeroth-order coefficient
is \(W\). This identifies every term generated by the extraction.

These are distributional identities in physical coordinates. Multiplication
by \(e^{\pm f}\) is locally admissible in \(H^2\): its weak Hessian contains
inverse distances, controlled by sliced Hardy and local cutoffs. The
previous extraction proves \(\mathcal R\in C^{1,1}_{\rm loc}\) and
\(\mathcal R-\psi_0=O(\rho^2)\). The function \(J\) has locally
square-integrable physical Hessian. Thus the product rules hold first off
collision sets and extend weakly across them, without surface
distributions. No all-order regularity assumption entered the derivation.

## Exact degeneracy and boundary strata

On the interior \(G\) is positive definite and
\[
\det G=\frac{abc(a+b+c)(r^2+s^2)}{u^2r^2s^2}.
\tag{PDE9}
\]
The open faces are collinear configurations with all distances nonzero:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
a=0&r=s+u\\
b=0&s=r+u\\
c=0&u=r+s
\end{array}
\]
At each such open face the matrix has rank two, with null direction equal
to that face's coordinate normal. These are coordinate degeneracies of
the orbit space, not physical boundary conditions.

The positive coordinate axes, which are intersections of two faces, are
the isolated pair collisions: the \(a\)-axis is \(r=0\), the \(b\)-axis is
\(s=0\), and the \(c\)-axis is \(u=0\). At those axes, \(\mathbf b\) is
singular. Some degree-zero rational coefficients have direction-dependent
limits. For example near the \(a\)-axis, \(a>0,\ b,c\to0\),
\(\mathsf A\sim c/(b+c)\). Thus ordinary coefficient analyticity across
the full closed octant is false even away from the vertex.

At the vertex all distances vanish. \(G,\mathbf t,W\) retain directional
degree-zero behavior; \(\mathbf b\) has degree \(-1\).
The actual geometry is a divergence-form operator in the weight \(w=rsu\)
with an orbit-space metric degenerating on the open faces and rational
directional coefficients on the axes. It is not the constant-coefficient
elliptic polyhedral boundary problem in standard weighted analytic theorems.
The extraction controls the actual solution's second derivatives; it does
not make the coefficients or the entire source analytic across an axis.

## Blow-up and every power of the scale

Let \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), write
\((a,b,c)=\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\), and put
\(\mathcal R_\varepsilon(z)=\widehat{\mathcal R}(\varepsilon z)\).
Use the same symbols \(S,T,q,f,G,\mathbf b,\mathbf t,W\) for their
expressions at \(z=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\). Work, for example, on
\[
\mathcal K=\{z\in[0,\infty)^3:1/2\le S(z)\le2\}.
\]
The rescalings are
\[
q(\varepsilon z)=\varepsilon^2q(z),\quad
T(\varepsilon z)=\varepsilon^2T(z),\quad
f(\varepsilon z)=\varepsilon f(z),\quad
w(\varepsilon z)=\varepsilon^3w(z),
\]
\[
G(\varepsilon z)=G(z),\quad
\mathbf b(\varepsilon z)=\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf b(z),\quad
\mathbf t(\varepsilon z)=\mathbf t(z),\quad W(\varepsilon z)=W(z).
\]
After multiplying the equation by \(\varepsilon^2\),
\[
\mathcal P_\varepsilon
=-\tfrac12\mathcal L_z-\varepsilon\mathbf t\cdot\nabla_z
+\varepsilon^2W.
\tag{PDE10}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_\varepsilon\mathcal R_\varepsilon
=\kappa\psi_0\{&
8\varepsilon^2q/T+2\varepsilon^3fq/T\\
&+[\varepsilon^3(-ZqS/(rs)-u/2)-\varepsilon^4Wq]
(2\log\varepsilon+\log T)\}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{PDE11}
\]
If the constant value is also subtracted,
\[
\mathcal P_\varepsilon(\mathcal R_\varepsilon-\psi_0)
=\text{right side of (PDE11)}-\varepsilon^2W\psi_0.
\tag{PDE12}
\]
On the real normalized physical cone the source is bounded by
\(C_Z\varepsilon^2(1+\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|)\).
Here \(T\ge S^2/2\), \(|q|\le rs\), and all displayed direction ratios
are bounded. On regions where \(r,s,u\ge\eta S\), its analytic norms on a
fixed complex neighborhood have the same uniform scale behavior.

Across a pair axis these ordinary analytic coefficient/source norms
are not available: for instance \(qS/(rs)\) has a directional limit.
The rescaled principal metric has the same degeneracy as before.
Simply declaring (PDE10) uniformly elliptic, or applying ordinary
analytic interior estimates through the axes, would therefore fail.

## An equivalent equation with a better analytic lift

The reduced equation must be derived as above, but it is not necessary
to estimate its nonanalytic individual coefficients. Set, in the
physical Cartesian annulus,
\[
U_\varepsilon(X_1,X_2)
=\frac{\psi_Z(\varepsilon X_1,\varepsilon X_2)-\psi_0}{\varepsilon}.
\]
The actual local Lipschitz bound on \(\psi_Z\) gives a uniform local
\(L^\infty\) bound for \(U_\varepsilon\). Its equation is
\[
[-\tfrac12\Delta+\varepsilon V-\varepsilon^2E]U_\varepsilon
=(-V+\varepsilon E)\psi_0.
\tag{PDE13}
\]
There is still one Coulomb singularity in an isolated-pair chart.
A Kustaanheimo–Stiefel lift clears it and gives a fixed Grushin principal
part with uniformly analytic lower-order terms and right side.
The accompanying uniform analytic proof treats this lifted equation.
This change of analytic proof coordinates is not a change of Hamiltonian,
target function, weight, or approximation dictionary.
