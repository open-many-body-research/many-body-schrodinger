## 2. Plain-English statement
For two electrons and a nucleus of charge $`Z`$ with $`9Z^2 > 32`$ (so $`Z \gtrsim 1.886`$, which includes He, Li⁺, Be²⁺, …):
- the ground state exists and is unique up to phase;
- its energy is below $`-5Z^2/8`$;
- every other point of the spectrum has real part at least $`-5Z^2/8`$.

For helium this means $`E_\text{He} < -2.5`$ Ha, and the rest of the spectrum lies at or above $`-2.5`$ Ha. That separator is exactly what the helium certificates ([S3-001](S3-001.md), [S3-002](S3-002.md)) use.

## 3. Hypotheses
$`Z>0`$ and $`9Z^2 > 32`$ define the scope; helium is fully instantiated. The comparison premise of [S2-001](S2-001.md) is **proved** here, not assumed.

## 4. Definitions audit
- The objects are those of [S1-001](S1-001.md) with $`N=2`$.
- $`s`$ is the explicit normalized hydrogenic singlet $`\propto e^{-Z(|x_1|+|x_2|)}`$ tensored with the spin singlet, constructed inside the fermionic space.

## 5. What this does NOT show
- It gives no numerical value of $`E`$ beyond $`E < -5Z^2/8`$.
- It does not cover H⁻ ($`Z=1`$), and it does not reach the range $`1.49 < Z \le 1.886`$ (open problem S2.4).
- The separator technique is classical (Bazley's intermediate problems).
