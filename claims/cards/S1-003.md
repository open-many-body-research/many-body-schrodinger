# S1-003: Hydrogen ground energy with spin

**Tier:** L  **Sector:** S1  **Status:** foundation  **Depends on:** none

## 1. Formal statement
`TheoremT.Continuum.hydrogen_full_spin_ground`, in [`HydrogenSpinGround_v1.lean`](../../lean/Foundation/HydrogenSpinGround_v1.lean):
```lean
theorem hydrogen_full_spin_ground (Z : ℝ) (hZ : 0 < Z) :
    variationalGroundEnergy 1 Z = ((-(Z ^ 2 / 2) : ℝ) : EReal) ∧
    formGroundEnergy 1 Z = ((-(Z ^ 2 / 2) : ℝ) : EReal) ∧
    spectralGroundEnergy 1 Z = ((-(Z ^ 2 / 2) : ℝ) : EReal) ∧
    Function.Injective (hydrogenSpinMap Z hZ) ∧
    (∀ c : Fin 2 → ℂ, hydrogenSpinMap Z hZ c ∈ targetDomain 1 ∧
      hamiltonianGraph 1 Z (hydrogenSpinMap Z hZ c)
        ((-(Z ^ 2 / 2) : ℂ) • hydrogenSpinMap Z hZ c)) ∧
    LinearIndependent ℂ (fun j : Fin 2 => hydrogenSpinMap Z hZ (Pi.basisFun ℂ (Fin 2) j))
```

Two related theorems are in [`HydrogenExactSolver_v1.lean`](../../lean/Foundation/HydrogenExactSolver_v1.lean):
- `hydrogenExactSolver_correct (Z p : ℕ)`: the returned rational interval encloses `spectralGroundEnergy 1 Z` and has width 0.
- `hydrogenExactCertificateCheck_sound`: every interval accepted by the Boolean checker encloses the ground energy.

## 2. Plain-English statement
For a hydrogen-like ion with nuclear charge $Z>0$, the variational, form and spectral ground energies are all exactly $-Z^2/2$ hartree. There are two linearly independent ground states (spin up and spin down). Both lie in the exact $H^2$ domain and satisfy the eigenvalue equation.

## 3. Hypotheses
Only $Z > 0$. For the solver, $Z$ is a natural number and $Z = 0$ is included.

## 4. Definitions audit
The operator and energies are those of [S1-001](S1-001.md) with $N = 1$. The eigenvector is written down explicitly (`hydrogenSpinMap`) and shown to be in the domain. Neither the eigenvector nor the eigenvalue is postulated.

## 5. What this does NOT show
- It does not show the multiplicity is *exactly* 2.
- The "solver" is the closed-form answer $-Z^2/2$, not a numerical method. What it contributes is the kernel-checked link to the actual continuum spectrum.
