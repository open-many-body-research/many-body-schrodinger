## 2. Plain-English meaning and derived inputs

A two-electron physical Coulomb eigenfunction has a common locally Lipschitz representative with the existing pointwise spin permutation law and Moser amplitude bound. On the fixed nuclear chart, outer initialization supplies actual first spectator weak derivatives. On the plateau, their differentiated physical equation supplies the inner Y, T, and YY derivatives. One common geometric constant bounds all three inner squared-norm sums by

```math
D(1+b_0^2+b_1^2)^2\,\mathcal M(2,Z,E)^2\lVert\psi\rVert^2,
\qquad b_0=\frac{26}{3}|Z|+\frac8{11}+\frac{|E|}{2},
\qquad b_1=\frac89|Z|+\frac{128}{121}.
```

Here `𝓜` is the existing `coulombMoserBoundCoefficient`. The norm is the original `SpinSpace 2` norm. No local derivative norm, coefficient bound, amplitude bound, or differentiated PDE is supplied as a new premise.

Writing `raw = u σ ∘ nuclearKSLift 0`, `H = 𝓜²‖ψ‖²`, and `U = χouter·raw` almost everywhere, the proof derives

```math
F_0=\int_{K_0}|\mathrm{raw}|^2\le\operatorname{vol}(K_0)H,
\qquad F=\int_K|d_j|^2\le C_0\operatorname{vol}(K_0)(1+b_0^2)H,
\qquad A=\int_K|U|^2\le\operatorname{vol}(K)H.
```

The first inequality follows pointwise from the spin square-sum Moser bound. The second uses the actual global L² first-T norm from outer initialization, restriction of a nonnegative integral, and the Lp norm-square/integral identity. The third uses `K ⊆ V` and `χouter = 1` on `V`, with equality almost everywhere transported to the restricted measure.

The literal forcing quantity is

```math
M=\int_K\bigl|-(D_{T_j}B)U-Bd_j\bigr|^2
\le2b_1^2A+2b_0^2F.
```

Actual coefficient and coefficient-derivative bounds discharge this inequality. With `s = 1+b0²+b1²`, the proof obtains `F+M ≤ 3(vol(K)+C0·vol(K0))s²H`; the displayed choice of `D` absorbs the three gain factors. These are proved estimates, not hypotheses in the public theorem.
